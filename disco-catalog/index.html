Music catalog vs DISCO inventory: matches, gaps, duplicates and versions

A master catalog spreadsheet and a DISCO track export go in. Out come the recordings DISCO is missing, the tracks the master does not know, duplicate uploads, alternate versions, WAV/MP3 pairs and the matches a person should check, plus the master sheet with DISCO Track IDs and links written back.

Status: The two files below are sample data for a fictional label, made for this demo, not client data. The DISCO file is a DISCO-style sample: column names to be matched to your real export. DISCO's help center says a track metadata export has a column for each standard field (Title, Artist, Album, ISRC, BPM and so on) and can include DISCO track IDs, but it does not publish the exact header (export guide, standard fields). Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Duplicate or a different version?

Same recordingLink it, don't delete it

Same ISRC. Or, when an ISRC is missing, the same title, the same artists, the same version label and a length within 1 second. Its WAV and MP3 are linked as formats. The same recording uploaded twice in the same format is a possible duplicate and stays until you approve removing it.

Different versionKeep it, link it to the song

A different version word (instrumental, clean, remix, radio edit, extended, acoustic, live), a different ISRC, or a length gap of more than 5 seconds. Each is its own recording, kept and grouped under the song.

Not sureAsk you, with the reason

A title that is close but not equal, an artist name spelled differently, or a length gap of 2 to 5 seconds with no ISRC to decide. These come with a score and are never merged on their own.

The master keeps every original column and row, with six columns added: DISCO Track ID, DISCO Link, DISCO Formats, DISCO Status, DISCO Match Rule and DISCO Notes. A second sheet lists every issue. The CSV holds the same issues, one row each, with a proposed action.

How matching works

  1. Clean the names. Artists are split on feat., ft., featuring, &, and, with, x and commas, then compared as a set with case and accents ignored, so Noé Dufresne = NOE DUFRESNE and A feat. B = A & B. A (feat. B) in the title moves to the artists.
  2. Pull out the version. Version words in brackets or after a dash in the title ((Inst.), - Radio Edit, (Kito Reyes Remix)) go into a version field together with any Version column, and synonyms are merged (Inst. = Instrumental, Original = no version).
  3. ISRC first. A DISCO track whose ISRC (dashes and case ignored) is in the master is matched to that row.
  4. Then title + artists + version + length. When one side has no ISRC: same cleaned title, same artists, same version and length within 1 second is a match. Same song but another version word, another ISRC or a length gap over 5 seconds is an alternate version. A gap of 2 to 5 seconds is uncertain.
  5. Then a fuzzy title score. If nothing above fits, every master title is scored against the DISCO title (letter-pair similarity, 70% title, 25% artists, 5% version). The best one at 75% or more is listed as uncertain, with its score; below that the track is "not in master".
  6. Group files of one recording. All DISCO tracks that landed on the same recording are grouped. The WAV (or the first lossless file) is the main one; an MP3 or other format is "formats linked"; a second file in the same format is a possible duplicate.
What was planted in the sample files, so you can check the tool
  1. 120 master recordings: 80 songs plus 40 alternate versions (instrumental, clean, remix, radio edit, extended).
  2. 104 of them are in DISCO. 92 share the ISRC (written with or without dashes, some in lower case; 3 of these have a title typo and 2 use "ft." in DISCO). 12 have no shared ISRC: "ft.", "&", "feat." in the title, all caps, accents dropped, "(Inst.)", or the ISRC only on the DISCO side (3 master rows have no ISRC).
  3. 10 master recordings are not in DISCO.
  4. 8 DISCO tracks are not in the master.
  5. 12 MP3 copies of WAVs already in DISCO (one MP3 listed before its WAV, one without an ISRC).
  6. 6 duplicate uploads: 5 with the same ISRC as another WAV, 1 with no ISRC but the same title, artists, version and length.
  7. 10 alternate versions not in the master: acoustic, remix, extended mix, live, instrumental, radio edit and clean versions of known songs, one track with a different ISRC but the same title and length, and two with a 28 s and a 41 s length gap.
  8. 6 uncertain: 4 title typos and 1 artist typo with no ISRC, and 1 track 3 seconds longer than its master row with no ISRC.
