What was wrong, and the fix

The cause of each miss was found by reading the chunks the old bot got. All fixes went in together; the next section measures each one.

  • Tables and sections cut in the middle (4 questions missed before, in both runs).
    Fix: heading- and table-aware chunking: one chunk per section, tables never split, each chunk tagged with its document and section title. After the full fix: 4 of 4 right, in both runs.
  • Outdated policy retrieved next to the current one, with no dates (3 questions missed before, in both runs).
    Fix: document metadata (effective date, current or superseded): outdated documents rank below current ones and are labelled in the prompt. After the full fix: 3 of 3 right, in both runs.
    Where the dates come from: each of the 12 test documents has its effective date and status (current or replaced) in its header, written when the documents were made. On real documents this comes from file dates, version names, or a one-time review that I do in the first step.
  • The right document was not in the top 2 results (3 questions missed before, in both runs).
    Fix: hybrid search (keywords + embeddings), a reranker, and 6 chunks instead of 2. After the full fix: 3 of 3 right, in both runs.
  • No sources and no “I don't know” rule in the prompt.
    Fix: every fact must cite its document, and the bot must say “The documents don't say.” when the answer is missing. After: 29 of 30 answers cite a source (before: 0). To be fair, the old bot already declined all 5 questions the documents don't answer, so that score did not move.

  • Whole tables matter for math. With the old chunks, the fixed bot fell to 27: the price row it needed was not in its chunks.

Three real questions, before and after

Table lookupWhat is the price per user of the Team plan when billed annually?

Before: Said “not in the documents” (it was)

Based on the provided context, the pricing details for the Team plan are missing/cut off from the table, so the exact price cannot be determined from the text.

After: Correct

The Team plan costs $20 per user per month when billed annually [pricing-plans]. Annual billing is charged once a year for the whole term [pricing-plans]. Calculation: $20 × 12 months = $240 per user per year [pricing-plans].

Why it changed: The 500-character split cut the pricing table inside its first row (Starter), so the Team row never reached the model. Keeping tables whole put the full table in the context. Measured: the old bot with 6 chunks instead of 2 also got this one right, in both runs, so more chunks alone fix it too.

Current vs outdated policyIs there a fee when a refund is paid back for a bank transfer payment?

Before: Wrong

Based on the provided text, there are conflicting policies:
• One section states: There is no fee for refunds, including refunds of payments made by bank transfer.
• Another section states: Refunds of payments made by bank transfer carry a $25 processing fee.

After: Correct

No, there is no fee for refunds, including refunds of payments made by bank transfer [refund-policy]. Approved refunds are sent to the original payment method within 5 business days [refund-policy].

Why it changed: The old 2024 refund policy and the current one were both retrieved, with no dates, so the bot offered both rules. With document dates and status, the outdated policy ranks below the current one and is labelled as superseded. Measured: without the dates and status, the fixed bot gave the old $25 fee as a second valid rule in 1 of 2 runs.

Multi-hop (two documents)A Business customer with 10 users on monthly billing had 98.5% uptime last month. How much is the service credit in dollars?

Before: Partly answered

Based on the provided context, the exact monthly price for the Business plan is missing (the plan comparison table cuts off after the Starter plan). Therefore, the exact dollar amount of the service credit cannot be determined.
However, based on the uptime table, an uptime of 98.5% qualifies for a 25% service credit (since it is below 99.0% but at least 95.0%) applied to that month's subscription fee.

After: Correct

The Business plan on monthly billing costs $39 per user per month [pricing-plans], making the monthly fee $390 for 10 users ($39 × 10 = $390). Because the Business uptime commitment is 99.9% [sla] and the monthly uptime of 98.5% is below 99.0% but at least 95.0%, the customer is eligible for a 25% service credit [sla].
Calculation: $390 × 0.25 = $97.50.
The service credit is $97.50, which is added to the account balance [sla].

Why it changed: The answer needs two documents: the price table and the SLA credit table. Before, the price table was cut off; after, both whole tables were retrieved and the bot showed the calculation. Measured: with the old 500-character chunks, the fixed bot still missed it in both runs.

One question got worse

“Which plans have an uptime SLA, and what is the API rate limit per minute on the cheapest of them?” The old bot got it right in both runs: Business, 1,000 requests per minute. The fixed bot missed it in both runs.

Both bots had the same two tables (uptime and API limits) and no prices. The fixed bot had no price table in its 6 chunks, and its rule is “never guess”, so it would not say which plan is cheaper. When the price table is in its chunks (question T7), it does answer. The old prompt guessed and happened to be right: with the old prompt on the same chunks, the fixed bot was also right in both runs.

No document says Business costs less than Enterprise (Enterprise is a custom quote), so the refusal is defensible. I still count it as a miss: 10 old misses fixed, 1 old pass lost, 20 → 29.

How it was tested

  • 30 questions with a known answer and source, written and saved in git before the first run: numbers inside tables, current vs outdated policy, answers needing two documents, exact numbers, and 5 questions the documents do not answer.
  • Grading: exact checks on key numbers and phrases first, then an LLM judge with a written rubric for the mixed cases. Then a second AI review, separate from the grader, checked 21 graded answers one by one against the documents: the grade (right or wrong) held for 21 of 21. For the one-change versions it checked 30 more: 30 of 30 held.
  • Before = a typical first build: fixed 500-character chunks, embedding search with the top 2 chunks, no dates, a plain prompt. After = section-based chunks with whole tables, keyword + embedding search, a reranker, document dates, and a prompt that must cite and may say “The documents don't say.”
  • Both use the same LLM (Gemini 3.8 Flash) and the same local embedding model. The whole demo, including the 4 main test runs, the 10 one-change runs and grading, cost $0.76 in LLM calls.
