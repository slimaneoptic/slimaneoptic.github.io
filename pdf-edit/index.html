Under the hood

  • Find the word

    pdf.js renders the page and locates the text run under your click (text content and operator list).

  • Rewrite the glyphs

    The word’s glyphs are replaced in the content stream. Tc, Tw and Tz are honoured, and widths come from the font’s own widths.

  • Shape Arabic

    HarfBuzz (WASM) shapes joining forms and lam-alef; bidi places digits and English inside Arabic. Right-to-left lines keep their right edge.

  • Choose the font

    First the embedded font itself, then glyphs already in its subset, then a bundled fallback (DejaVu Sans, Arabic and Latin) embedded as a subset. Standard PDF fonts that are not embedded (Helvetica, Times, Courier) are written in their own encoding.

  • Keep it searchable

    Every new letter gets its own ToUnicode text, so the new word extracts, searches and copies correctly. Where a font draws dots or hamza as separate glyphs, those dots are drawn as outlines from the same font.

  • Leave punctuation alone

    A period, an Arabic comma or brackets next to a word are not part of the selection, so they stay in place. The file is saved with pdf-lib.

Limits of this demo

  • Not edited: text inside form XObjects, Type3 fonts, encrypted PDFs, vertical or rotated text, text without a Unicode mapping (the demo says so instead of guessing).
  • Typed harakat (tashkeel) use the fallback font when the subset has no mark glyphs.
  • A longer word moves the rest of the line; nothing wraps, and moved words can slide under boxes drawn later (highlights, table fills).
  • Tested in Chromium and Firefox, at desktop and phone size; Safari is not tested yet.
