Self-storage revenue model for small rural facilities

Weekly street rates per unit size from local competitor rates, your occupancy and your own move-in season, with a plain-English reason for every number. Plus the list of existing tenants who are due a rate increase, and who to skip.

Status: Sample data, fictional: a 175-unit facility and 12 invented rural competitors, in the shapes of a StorTrack export and a rent roll (column order assumed; the column match is shown and can be changed). The rules are taken from the client brief; every threshold is an editable default. Everything runs in your browser.

How it works

  1. Comp set. For each unit size, the newest non-climate web rate per competitor, and a call-around price where a competitor posts none. Competitors are put in 0-3, 3-10 and 10-25 mile rings; farther ones are left out.
  2. Target. Occupancy picks the tier and the tier picks a percentile. Close comps weigh more: with weights 3/2/1 a 0-3 mile rate is counted three times, then the percentile is taken like Excel's PERCENTILE.INC.
  3. Season. Each month's average weekly move-ins divided by the all-year average gives a factor, averaged 1-2-1 with the months either side because a small facility has few move-ins a month. A share of that swing (capped) moves the target.
  4. Guardrails. If close comps' median rate fell more than the hold limit over the last weeks, the rate holds. Otherwise small moves inside the dead band are skipped and the weekly change is capped. Rates are whole dollars.
  5. ECRI. Each tenant is checked in order: days late, tenure, last increase, rent at or above street, gap too small. Eligible tenants get a share of the gap to the street rate, capped, never above street.
