Flywheel for a 1.4-3 MJ pulse inside 1500-1550 RPM: a first-pass sizing

Two ways to absorb the pulse with the speed rising by at most 50 RPM: a conventional fixed flywheel, and a variable-inertia one where masses move outward against a spring. Hand calculation in Python, checked a second way in the same script. This is a screening estimate, not a design.

Inputs (from the brief)

  • Pulse energy: 1.4 MJ to 3 MJ
  • Speed band: 1500 to 1550 RPM (157.1 to 162.3 rad/s)

Assumptions (mine, to be replaced by your numbers)

  • Steel, density 7850 kg/m³, allowable rim stress 400 MPa (assumption: includes margin for fatigue; real value needs the actual alloy and load cycles). Poisson ratio 0.29.
  • Fixed flywheel: thin rim at 0.60 m mean radius, or solid disc of the same radius.
  • Variable inertia: masses travel from 0.30 m to 0.60 m; one linear spring with preload; no friction, massless guides, no other rotor inertia, quasi-static motion. Speed is exactly 1500 RPM at 0.30 m and 1550 RPM at 0.60 m.

What this says

  • A fixed flywheel can do the job, but it is heavy: about 4.7 to 10 t as a thin rim at 0.60 m. Stress is low (74 MPa), so the limit is mass, not strength.
  • Moving masses cut the moving mass by roughly 24 times in this model, because the spring takes about half of the energy.
  • The cost is in the spring: 1.5 to 6.7 MN, which is the high-cycle fatigue question.
  • The stability margin is only 6%. A 50 RPM band means the centrifugal curve and the spring line are nearly parallel. Small errors in preload, friction or mass can give hunting or snap-through. A tuned spring (progressive or nonlinear) is the first thing to study.

Formulas

  • Fixed: E = ½ I (w2² - w1²); thin rim I = m r²; disc I = ½ m r²; rim stress = rho v²; disc centre stress = (3+nu)/8 rho v².
  • Variable: balance m w² r = F0 + k (r - r1) at both ends; E = ½ m (w2² r2² - w1² r1²) + F0 d + ½ k d², with d = r2 - r1.
  • Check: the energy is integrated numerically along the equilibrium path (20,001 points) and agrees with the formula; the script also asserts that the speed stays inside 1500-1550 RPM along the whole travel.

What a full study adds

  • Your real geometry, mass and space limits, pulse duration and torque source
  • Nonlinear spring options and the exact force curve against speed
  • Dynamic stability: response time against pulse length, damping, hunting
  • Fatigue life of spring and guides (stress range, material, cycles)
  • Synchronization of several masses, balance, bearing loads, critical speeds
  • An editable calculation sheet with every equation
