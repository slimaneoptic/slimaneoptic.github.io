Fraud risk score for mobile-money transfers (0–100)

Every transfer gets a score from 0 to 100 and the three reasons behind it, in plain words. Trained on 5 weeks of transactions, calibrated on the 6th, and tested on the last 2 weeks, which it had never seen: 22,063 transfers, 256 of them fraud.

What moves the score most

  1. First transfer to this recipient
  2. How new the recipient's account is
  3. Amount compared with the sender's usual
  4. Time since the last SIM swap
  5. Amount
  6. How long the sender has been active

What it misses, honestly

25 fraudulent transfers scored under 20. 18 of them are scams where the victims sent the money themselves, so there was no SIM swap or new device to see. The next signals to add for those: a recipient suddenly receiving money from many new senders (a typical mule pattern), a call in progress during the transfer, and confirmed fraud reports fed back into training.

How it works, for non-technical readers

  • It only uses what is known at the moment of the transfer: the sender's own history up to that second, never the future.
  • It learns patterns from past confirmed cases: for example, a SIM swap followed within hours by a new device and a large transfer to a brand-new account.
  • The score is calibrated: it tracks the real chance of fraud. In testing, 97.4% of transfers scored 60 or more were fraud, against 0.1% of those under 20.
  • Every score comes with its reasons, so an analyst can see why, and customers can be given a clear explanation.
  • It is tested on later weeks it never saw, the same way it would face next month's transactions.

Synthetic data generated for this demo: 60 days, 4,000 users, about 88,000 transfers, with account takeovers, scams and legitimate look-alikes (real SIM swaps, new phones, rent-sized payments). Real data will change the numbers. Python, pandas, XGBoost, isotonic calibration, SHAP.
